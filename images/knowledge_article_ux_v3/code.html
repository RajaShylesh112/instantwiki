Enceladus Subsurface Ocean

schedule Last indexed 2 days ago
verified AI Verified Content
Knowledge Density Advanced
Associations 14 Linked Pages
Primary Relation Astrobiology

A global reservoir of liquid water located beneath the icy crust of Saturn's sixth-largest moon, Enceladus is considered one of the most promising environments for extraterrestrial life. Data from the Cassini-Huygens mission has confirmed that complex organic molecules are present in the plumes of water vapor and ice grains erupting from the moon.

Overview of Composition

The presence of ice grains, rich in sodium and potassium, suggests that the ocean is in direct contact with a rocky core, facilitating rich chemical interactions. Researchers postulate that the tidal heating generated by Saturn's gravitational pull maintains the ocean's temperature.

The Tiger Stripes & Plumes

The southern polar region of Enceladus features four prominent linear depressions known as "Tiger Stripes." These fractures serve as the exit points for cryogenic geysers that eject ocean material into space, forming the E-ring of Saturn. Recent analysis indicates that these plumes contain molecular hydrogen (H2), a strong indicator of hydrothermal activity.

Chemical Potential for Life

  • Presence of CH4 (Methane) in plume concentrations exceeding geochemical models.
  • Dissolved carbonates indicating a pH level between 11 and 12 (alkaline).
  • High concentrations of Silica (SiO2) particles suggesting active hydrothermal vents.

# Sarah the Strategist - Tertiary Persona

> Tertiary target - Validates the B2B commercial feasibility and private wiki monetization path.

**Priority:** TERTIARY 🏠
**Role in Flywheel:** Sarah is the commercial driver. She discovers the tool through public wikis shared by developers like Dan. She adopts it to build private, internal wikis (e.g. *Competitor Audits*, *User Research Repositories*) for her startup team, eventually upgrading to the Pro plan for team access and private wikis.
**Created:** 2026-06-08

---

## Profile Summary

Sarah is a Product Manager at a fast-growing startup. She conducts extensive competitor audits, customer interviews, and market research. She saves her findings in Google Docs, PDFs, and Notion databases, but her team rarely reads them—they get lost in Slack or Google Drive folders. She wants to turn this raw data into a central, highly searchable internal knowledge wiki where developers, designers, and marketers can quickly look up competitor pricing, user quotes, or product specs.

By addressing Sarah's needs for privacy, global document search, and traceable references, we create a clear path to subscription monetization.

## Current Situation

### Professional Reality

**The Daily Struggle:**
- **Knowledge Silos:** Team members ask repetitive questions (e.g., *"What does Competitor X charge for feature Y?"*) even though Sarah wrote a 20-page competitor report on it last month.
- **Google Drive Black Hole:** Documents, transcript files, and competitive analysis PDFs are uploaded to folders but never opened again.
- **Manual Formatting:** Spends hours summarizing interviews into Notion pages, formatting headers, and copy-pasting tables.

**Skills & Tools:**
- **High Skills:** User research, product roadmap planning, Notion databases, Slack search.
- **Low Skills:** Data parsing scripting, web building tools.
- **Learning Attitude:** Very pragmatic. Will only use tools that immediately save time or improve team alignment.

**The Search Gap:**
- Sarah's team needs answers, not more documents. Standard search engines in Notion or Drive don't search inside uploaded PDF attachments well.
- She needs a system that digests PDFs/links and builds a searchable website where answers can be looked up instantly.

## Driving Forces

### ✅ Top 3 Positive Drivers (What Sarah Wants)

**1. Team Search Adoption**
- Sarah wants her team to actually go to the wiki, search for a term, and get the answers they need without DM'ing her on Slack.
- *Why it matters:* Saves her hours of answering repetitive questions and improves the speed of decision-making.
- *What success looks like:* A developer checking the competitor wiki to verify a pricing tier before writing code.
- **Instant Wiki Promise:** The prominent Wiki Search Bar is optimized to search concepts, pages, and source text.

**2. Traceable Trust & Citations**
- Sarah wants her summaries to link back to the exact page of the uploaded PDFs so the engineering team trusts the facts.
- *Why it matters:* Engineers are skeptical of AI summaries. They need to verify that a quote or spec is 100% accurate.
- *What success looks like:* An engineer clicking a summary page citation and reading the original PDF sentence.
- **Instant Wiki Promise:** The Wiki Article page shows reference links mapping directly back to source PDFs.

**3. Single Source of Truth**
- Sarah wants a single website link she can send to new hires, designers, and marketing to explain the competitor landscape.
- *Why it matters:* Streamlines onboarding and keeps the entire company aligned on strategy.
- *What success looks like:* Sharing a single link `instant.wiki/u/startup/competitor-hub` in the onboarding document.
- **Instant Wiki Promise:** Simple slug customization and clean wiki landing pages serve as a unified hub.

---

### ❌ Top 3 Negative Drivers (What Sarah Fears)

**1. Confidential IP Leakage**
- Sarah fears that her competitor research, customer interviews, or startup strategies will leak publicly on the internet.
- *Why this terrifies her:* Compromising company secrets could damage her startup or breach customer confidentiality.
- *What failure looks like:* A Google search result showing a private research page from her wiki.
- **Instant Wiki Answer:** Robust visibility settings allow setting wikis to `PRIVATE` or `UNLISTED`, blocking search engines and requiring login to view.

**2. Formatting Cleanup Overhead**
- Sarah fears spending her entire day fixing broken tables, scrambled lists, or weird markdown layouts after the AI parses her PDFs.
- *Why this terrifies her:* If she has to spend hours cleaning up the site, she might as well write it manually in Notion.
- *What failure looks like:* Extracted text showing overlapping characters or scrambled columns.
- **Instant Wiki Answer:** Document parsers extract clean structured markdown, keeping layout cleanup to a minimum.

**3. Tool Abandonment**
- Sarah fears spending time setting up the wiki only for the team to ignore it and continue asking questions on Slack.
- *Why this terrifies her:* Wasted strategic effort and money.
- *What failure looks like:* Active user count on the wiki dropping to zero after the first week.
- **Instant Wiki Answer:** Elegant layouts and interactive graphs encourage exploration, while the search bar provides instant utility.

# Dan the Developer - Secondary Persona

> Secondary target - Drives organic viral growth and referral loops by publishing and sharing high-quality public wikis.

**Priority:** SECONDARY 💼
**Role in Flywheel:** Dan acts as the marketing and sharing engine. He creates high-quality technical wikis (e.g., *Machine Learning Atlas*, *System Design Notes*) and shares them on Twitter, GitHub, and his portfolio. Each of his public pages displays a prominent "Built with Instant Wiki" badge, converting his readers into new users.
**Created:** 2026-06-08

---

## Profile Summary

Dan is a Full-Stack Software Engineer who loves side projects and learning new technical concepts. He reads constantly and maintains a large local vault of Markdown notes (using Obsidian). He wants to publish a beautiful "digital garden" website of his notes to showcase his expertise on Twitter or on his resume. However, he hates wasting hours configuring Astro, Docusaurus, or Tailwind styling, and he finds Obsidian Publish too expensive.

By providing Dan with a fast, beautiful, and effortless markdown-to-website pipeline, we leverage his public sharing habit to drive organic user acquisition.

- **Learning Attitude:** Loves trying new developer tools, but has low tolerance for clunky, slow interfaces or "dumb" AI bots.

**The Publishing Gap:**
- Dan has high-quality content (his markdown files) and a desire to publish, but the setup friction of static site generators (SSGs) stops him.
- He needs a service that builds the site instantly and renders a premium interface, including a beautiful interactive graph view.

## Driving Forces

### ✅ Top 3 Positive Drivers (What Dan Wants)

**1. Social Proof & Reputation Building**
- Dan wants a polished, professional-looking website link he can proudly pin to his Twitter bio or add to his resume.
- *Why it matters:* Helps him stand out for job opportunities and builds his personal brand in the developer community.
- *What success looks like:* A tweet sharing `instant.wiki/dan/ml-atlas` getting likes and retweets.
- **Instant Wiki Promise:** Public wikis feature high-quality styling (Notion + Wikipedia aesthetic) with clean typography.

**2. Dynamic Graph Navigation**
- Dan wants a full-screen interactive node graph that users can zoom, pan, and click to explore his notes.
- *Why it matters:* It looks incredibly slick and makes his personal wiki stand out from standard flat blogs.
- *What success looks like:* A viewer clicking around the interactive graph to read different concept pages.
- **Instant Wiki Promise:** The full-screen Graph Page provides zoom, pan, search, and direct page routing on node clicks.

**3. Frictionless Markdown Import**
- Dan wants to upload a folder of markdown files and have the wiki render them immediately.
- *Why it matters:* He doesn't want to copy-paste page by page or write custom routing scripts.
- *What success looks like:* Dragging 20 markdown files into the upload box and seeing them live in 10 seconds.
- **Instant Wiki Promise:** The upload module natively parses markdown files, extracting headings, cross-links, and categories.

---

### ❌ Top 3 Negative Drivers (What Dan Fears)

**1. Cheap "AI Wrapper" Stigma**
- Dan fears his website looking like a generic ChatGPT copy-paste or a basic chatbot template, which would look unprofessional on his resume.
- *Why this terrifies him:* It hurts his reputation as a skilled developer who builds custom things.
- *What failure looks like:* A site featuring a massive chat interface with a placeholder look.
- **Instant Wiki Answer:** The design language uses zero chat-bubbles. It is presented as a GitBook/Obsidian-style publishing portal with clean serif typography.

**2. Platform Lock-in**
- Dan fears that once he builds his wiki on the platform, his notes will be locked in and he won't be able to export them.
- *Why this terrifies him:* As a developer, he values ownership of his text data and portability.
- *What failure looks like:* No export button, requiring manual copying of 50 pages if he wants to leave.
- **Instant Wiki Answer:** The platform supports full Markdown ZIP export, letting Dan take his files whenever he wants.

**3. Slow Loading & Bloat**
- Dan fears that the generated site will be slow to load, bloated with JavaScript, or perform poorly on mobile.
- *Why this terrifies him:* Bad performance is a red flag for developers and hurts SEO/rankings.
- *What failure looks like:* A 5-second loading spinner before the graph renders.
- **Instant Wiki Answer:** Lightweight server-rendered Next.js pages and optimized SVG/Canvas graph render libraries ensure fast load times.
